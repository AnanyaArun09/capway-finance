College costs can be confusing. Let’s make them clearer.

CapWay is a student-friendly guide for making sense of college costs and financial aid. Use it to compare the yearly costs and aid offers from up to five colleges, see how grants, scholarships, loans, and work-study differ, and estimate what a loan payment might look like.

You can start with the information you already have. CapWay does not look up or verify college prices or aid offers, so use each school’s official information and ask its financial aid office to confirm your numbers.

Loans must be repaid, and work-study must be earned; neither reduces the college’s cost. Confirm costs, eligibility, and current award details with each college.

Questions to ask each college’s financial aid office

  • What costs are included in this yearly estimate, and what might be extra?
  • Which grants and scholarships can renew next year, and what do I need to do to keep them?
  • Which loans are offered, what are their interest rates, and do I have to accept them?
  • How does work-study pay me, and how many hours might I need to work?
  • After grants and scholarships, what amount would my family still need to plan for?

02 · Know what kind of help it is

“Financial aid” can mean different things.

Some help reduces what you owe. Other options help you pay over time—or are money you must repay. Check your actual offer before deciding.

Usually does not need to be repaid

Grants and scholarships

Money for school that usually doesn’t have to be paid back. Some awards have rules, such as keeping certain grades or enrolling in a specific program. Read the offer carefully.

Learn about grants on StudentAid.gov ↗
Earned through a job

Work-study and student jobs

This is money you earn by working. It is not usually taken off your bill in advance, and you need to work the hours to receive the pay.

Learn about work-study ↗
Must be repaid

Student loans

A loan is borrowed money. You pay it back, often with extra money called interest. Before accepting, check the interest rate, fees, and repayment rules.

Learn about federal student loans ↗
A way to cover the rest

Savings, family help, and payment plans

These may help cover costs left after grants or scholarships. Ask the college about payment-plan details and any fees before choosing one.

04 · A next-step checklist

You don’t have to figure it all out today.

Use these steps to replace guesses with information from the people and schools that can confirm it.

  1. 1

    Find each school’s yearly cost estimate

    Look for “cost of attendance” or “net price calculator” on the school’s official website. Include living costs—not just tuition.

  2. 2

    Apply for financial aid if you plan to

    Use the official FAFSA website and ask your school counselor or the college’s financial aid office if you need help.

    Go to the official FAFSA site ↗
  3. 3

    Check scholarships on the college’s own website

    Look for deadlines, who can apply, what the award covers, and whether it can be renewed. Never assume you’ll receive an award until it is offered to you.

  4. 4

    Read the aid offer line by line

    Separate grants and scholarships from work-study and loans. Ask the college to explain anything you don’t understand before accepting.

  5. 5

    Ask questions—this is what the aid office is there for

    Try: “What will I still need to pay for the year?” “Which aid do I have to repay?” “What costs or fees are not included here?”
